Job Market Paper

“Place-Based Policy Through Land: Firm Selection, Spatial Misallocation, and Rural-Urban Redistribution”

Draft coming soon

Abstract

This paper studies how the preferential allocation of urban land to industrial use affects productivity, output and welfare through firm selection and rural-urban migration, using China's primary urban land market as an empirical setting. The data suggests that prefectures where industrial land is cheaper allocate more land and employment to manufacturing and sustain more firms, yet have lower average productivity and only modestly higher manufacturing output. In particular, the discounted industrial land sustains marginal unproductive firms and weakens selection, with rural migrants account for much of the employment growth. The paper then develops a spatial equilibrium model with heterogenous firms, rural-urban migration and land allocation to quantify the aggregate and distributional impacts of land price wedge. Eliminating within-prefecture land price wedge reduces manufacturing output by 5.2% but raises average productivity by about 7%, which are primarily drive by improved firm selection. The welfare impacts are distributional: urban welfare increases by 2.3%, driven mainly by lower housing prices, whereas rural welfare falls by 0.6% as urban manufacturing labor demand contracts. Furthermore, the inefficiency lies primarily in where industrial land is allocated rather than its aggregate share. These findings imply that evaluating place-based industrial policy requires considering not only output and employment, but also which firms produce, where production occurs, and who gains or loses.
